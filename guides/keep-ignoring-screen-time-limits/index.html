How to Stop Giving Yourself More Screen Time on iPhone

By Return’s maker · Updated 24 November 2026

The next time your iPhone says you’ve reached your Screen Time limit, ask yourself, “What did I open this app to finish?” If you need more time to reply, send the message and close the app. If you’ve already finished, lock the phone before another post catches your eye. If the limit never appears at all, start with Apple’s Screen Time setup guide.

Does the limit cover an app you actually need?

Suppose you meant to spend less time on Instagram, but your limit covers several social apps. Catching up with family in a separate messaging app can use up the same allowance. Check which apps you grouped together. A limit on Instagram alone may fit the choice you wanted to make.

If both the messages and the feed you use are inside Instagram, changing the app list won’t separate them. Before you open Instagram again, decide when you’ll close it. Sending the photo your sister asked for gives you a clear place to stop. If getting into the feed starts with an accidental tap, try moving Instagram off your Home Screen.

Should I change my daily limit or set a schedule?

Choose the setting that fits when you want to stop. For “half an hour of Instagram across the day,” look for a daily allowance. For “keep Instagram off during dinner,” look for a schedule.

  • If you see App Limits in Settings → Screen Time, open it to review the apps and time you selected. For a recurring break such as dinner, iOS 26 has a separate Downtime setting. With a Screen Time passcode set, you can turn on Block at Downtime for those hours. Apple’s iOS 26 instructions.
  • If you see Time Allowances, open it to review your daily budget. Screen Time Schedule lets you choose which app categories are available during a time window. Check Always Allowed for apps and people you need to reach. Apple’s guide to Time Allowances and Schedules.

Write down why you wanted to stop scrolling today

Think of one thing you missed while you were scrolling today. Write it down in Notes or a journal app you already use. A sentence like “I wanted to call my sister while she was walking home, but by the time I closed Instagram she was already home” gives tomorrow’s decision some context.

Pick the moment to read it again. If you usually call her after dinner, open the note when you clear your plate. If paper is easier, leave a slip by the sink. Tomorrow, the question can be as simple as “Do I have time to call her now?”

If you’d prefer a prompt partway through a visit, the iPhone guide to timers and check-ins explains the options.
