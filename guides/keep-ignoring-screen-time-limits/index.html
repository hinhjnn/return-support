How to Stop Ignoring Screen Time Limits

By Return’s maker · Reviewed 9 November 2026

If you keep ignoring Screen Time limits, start with your last override. Did the allowance interrupt something necessary, did you forget your reason for stopping, or do you want a restriction that is harder to override? Those are different needs. Choose the adjustment that matches yours.

Choose the kind of boundary you need

  • The allowance does not fit necessary use. Review which activities it covers and whether the duration is realistic.
  • You want to remember your intention. A message at entry or a check-in during a visit may give you a useful moment to choose.
  • You want stronger enforcement. Review Apple’s passcode and scheduled restriction controls. A reminder that lets you continue may not meet that requirement.

This guide concerns limits you set for yourself on your own phone. A limit that never appears is a settings problem to investigate separately. Start with Apple’s Screen Time setup guidance.

Use one recent override as the starting point

An app may hold work, conversations and entertainment. An allowance that covers all three may need more thought than a smaller number chosen in frustration.

If you want a harder restriction

Apple’s iOS 26 guide describes App Limits for daily allowances. It also explains using a Screen Time passcode with Block at Downtime for a scheduled restriction. Review which apps and contacts must remain available before changing it. Apple’s iOS 26 scheduling instructions.

Do not assume a limit is impossible to override because it has a passcode. Check how your own setup handles exceptions and recovery. Apple’s newer guides may use different labels; follow the instructions for your iOS version.

Return’s personal-reminder experience includes a choice to continue. It is designed for reconsidering that choice and may not fit someone who wants to remove it.

Give an extension a purpose and an end

“Finish replying to Alex” is a concrete endpoint. If you choose to browse, you might start a timer and put the phone down when it ends. A timer is useful only if you notice it; adding more alerts is not automatically a better solution.

A daily allowance measures a different thing from a prompt during one visit. The timer and check-in guide explains both. A Clock timer keeps counting after you leave Instagram.

Have a realistic next step ready: lunch on the table, a film you have chosen, a book beside your seat, or a few quiet minutes. If remembering your intention is the problem, a sentence such as “I want enough of tonight left to cook and call my sister” can sit in a note you revisit.

If remembering your reason is what you want

I make Return. Before Apps brings back a personal reminder, called an Echo, before a selected app and lets you choose to continue. While Using can keep an Echo available in a supported Lock Screen or Live Activity presentation, with optional usage check-ins.

The timer measures elapsed Return-session time, not exact time spent inside Instagram or another app.

Selected-app features need Screen Time permission; notifications need separate permission. iOS controls delivery timing and Live Activity presentation. An exact alert interval or continuous Dynamic Island display is not promised.
